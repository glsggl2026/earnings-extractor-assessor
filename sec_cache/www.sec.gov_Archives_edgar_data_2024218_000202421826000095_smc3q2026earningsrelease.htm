Summit Midstream Corporation Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial and Operating Results
Houston, Texas (November 9, 2026) – Summit Midstream Corporation (NYSE: SMC) (“Summit”, “SMC” or the “Company”) announced today its financial and operating results for the three months ended September 30, 2026.
Highlights
•Third quarter 2026 net income of $15.9 million, Adjusted EBITDA of $70.7 million, an increase of 16% relative to the second quarter of 2026, cash flow available for distributions (“Distributable Cash Flow” or “DCF”) of $32.6 million and free cash flow (“FCF”) of $5.2 million
•Raised 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance range to $250 million to $260 million, from $235 million to $255 million, amid strong producer activity and growing Rockies volumes, and expect a year-end 2026 total net leverage ratio of approximately 3.6x1
•Reached final investment decision on the Double E Mainline Compression Expansion, further positioning Summit to serve growing Permian Basin customer demand
•Double E throughput increased 17% to a record 1,002 MMcf/d relative to the second quarter of 2026, and the pipeline remains on track to fully subscribe the remaining forward-haul capacity by year-end
•Rockies Segment Adjusted EBITDA increased 32% to $40.0 million relative to the second quarter of 2026, driven by a 40% increase in liquids volume throughput
•Mid-Con Segment Adjusted EBITDA increased 7% to $22.8 million relative to the second quarter of 2026, driven by a 7% increase in natural gas volume throughput to 561 MMcf/d
Management Commentary
“The third quarter was one of the strongest in Summit’s history, driven by an active customer base and a supportive commodity price environment. Fifty-one new wells came online across the Williston and DJ Basins, driving a 40% increase in liquids volumes and a 32% increase in Rockies Segment Adjusted EBITDA. Combined with continued growth in our Permian and Mid-Con Segments, total Adjusted EBITDA reached $70.7 million, up 16% from the prior quarter. Since the first quarter, new well connects, volume growth and favorable commodity prices have added roughly $16.5 million of quarterly Adjusted EBITDA, approximately 30% growth in just two quarters. That momentum gives us the confidence to raise our full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance range to $250 million to $260 million.
“On the commercial front, we reached a positive final investment decision on the Double E Mainline Compression Expansion, and volumes were at record levels during the quarter. Both reflect the growing reliance on Double E to meet in-basin gas transportation needs in the Permian. We continue to make great progress securing additional firm transportation contracts and remain on track to subscribe the remaining expansion capacity to Waha by year-end.
“Looking ahead, we are beginning to see a real step-change in the Company’s organic growth outlook over the next two to three years, driven primarily by our Rockies and Permian Segments. With seven rigs running behind our systems and approximately 140 DUCs, we currently expect to connect 200 to 250 wells in 2027, which will drive meaningful year-over-year volume and EBITDA growth. Our Rockies producers are also signaling plans to continue to grow activity levels and volumes well into 2028 and beyond. In the Permian, we’re seeing demand for in-basin gas transportation services on Double E not only filling up the Mainline Compression Expansion capacity to Waha but also utilizing the bidirectional capability of the system to access additional markets and outlets on the northern end of the pipeline. The combination of the strong growth outlook in the Rockies and Permian Segments, along with a steady outlook in the Mid-Con, could drive a mid-to-high-teens compound annual growth rate in Adjusted EBITDA over the next several years. We’re really excited about the momentum we’re seeing in the business and the

Third Quarter 2026 Business Highlights
SMC's average daily natural gas throughput on its wholly owned, operated systems increased 5.9% to 952 MMcf/d, while liquids volumes increased 39.7% to 95 Mbbl/d, relative to the second quarter of 2026. Double E Pipeline averaged 1,002 MMcf/d and contributed $11.2 million in Adjusted EBITDA, net to SMC, for the third quarter of 2026.
Natural gas price-driven segments:
•Natural gas price-driven segments generated $31.1 million in combined Segment Adjusted EBITDA, a $1.0 million increase relative to the second quarter of 2026, with combined capital expenditures of $5.3 million
•Mid-Con Segment Adjusted EBITDA totaled $22.8 million, an increase of approximately $1.4 million relative to the second quarter of 2026, primarily due to a 7.3% increase in natural gas volume throughput to 561 MMcf/d, reflecting a full quarter of contribution from wells connected in the second quarter across both the Barnett and Arkoma systems. There is currently one rig running in the Barnett.
•Piceance Segment Adjusted EBITDA totaled $8.3 million, a decrease of $0.4 million relative to the second quarter of 2026, as higher operating expenses offset a 4.2% increase in volume throughput to 223 MMcf/d. There were no new wells connected to the system during the quarter.
Oil price-driven segments:
•Oil price-driven segments generated $51.1 million in combined Segment Adjusted EBITDA, a $11.4 million increase relative to the second quarter of 2026, with combined capital expenditures of $15.0 million
•Rockies Segment Adjusted EBITDA totaled $40.0 million, an increase of $9.6 million relative to the second quarter of 2026, driven by a 39.7% increase in liquids volume throughput. During the quarter, 51 new wells were connected to the Rockies systems, 32 in the Williston Basin and 19 in the DJ Basin, including 9 wells in the Williston for which Summit provides both crude oil and produced water gathering services, which meaningfully contributed to the uplift in liquids volumes. Third quarter results also include the benefit of approximately $3.7 million of commercial billing settlements. Six rigs are currently running in the Rockies Segment, including four in the Williston Basin and two in the DJ Basin, with approximately 140 DUCs behind the systems.
•Permian Segment Adjusted EBITDA totaled $11.2 million, an increase of approximately $1.8 million relative to the second quarter of 2026, driven by new contracts placed into service during the quarter and a 16.6% increase in Double E volume throughput to 1,002 MMcf/d.

These projects are underpinned by customer development plans and are expected to contribute to volume and Adjusted EBITDA growth in 2027.

Capital & Liquidity
As of September 30, 2026, SMC had $12.5 million in unrestricted cash-on-hand and $80 million drawn under its $500 million ABL Revolver with $417 million of borrowing availability, after accounting for $2.7 million of issued, but undrawn letters of credit. As of September 30, 2026, SMC was in compliance with all financial covenants, including interest coverage of 3.0x relative to a minimum interest coverage covenant of 2.0x and first lien net leverage ratio of 0.3x relative to a maximum first lien net leverage ratio of 2.5x. As of September 30, 2026, SMC reported a total net leverage ratio of approximately 3.9x.
As defined in the ABL credit agreement, the total net leverage ratio is calculated as the consolidated debt of Summit Midstream Holdings, LLC and its restricted subsidiaries, net of up to $50 million of unrestricted cash, divided by trailing twelve-month EBITDA as defined in the agreement. Summit Permian Transmission Holdco, LLC and Summit Permian Transmission, LLC are unrestricted subsidiaries under the agreement. Accordingly, the Permian Transmission Term Loan is excluded from debt, and credit agreement EBITDA excludes SMC’s proportional share of Double E’s Adjusted EBITDA and instead includes, subject to certain limitations, cash distributions received from Double E. Credit agreement EBITDA may also include adjustments for material capital projects and other items permitted under the agreement.
As of September 30, 2026, the Summit Permian Transmission Term Loan Facility had a balance of $360 million. Summit Midstream Permian has $9.2 million of cash-on-hand as of September 30, 2026. The Permian Transmission Term Loan remains non-recourse to SMC.
MVC Shortfall Payments
SMC billed its customers $4.1 million in the third quarter of 2026 related to MVC shortfalls. For those customers that do not have MVC shortfall credit banking mechanisms in their gathering agreements, the MVC shortfall payments are accounted for as gathering revenue in the period in which they are earned. In the third quarter of 2026, SMC recognized $4.1 million of gathering revenue associated with MVC shortfall payments. SMC had $0.0 million of adjustments to MVC shortfall payments in the third quarter of 2026. SMC’s MVC shortfall payment mechanisms contributed $4.1 million of total Adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter of 2026. The minimum volume commitments underpinning SMC's Piceance segment gathering agreements, which represent substantially all of SMC's MVC shortfall payments, expired at the end of the third quarter of 2026.

Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures
We report financial results in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”). We also present adjusted EBITDA, segment adjusted EBITDA, Distributable Cash Flow, and Free Cash Flow, non-GAAP financial measures.
Adjusted EBITDA
We define adjusted EBITDA as net income or loss, plus interest expense, income tax expense, depreciation and amortization, our proportional adjusted EBITDA for equity method investees, adjustments related to MVC shortfall payments, adjustments related to capital reimbursement activity, share-based and noncash compensation, impairments, items of income or loss that we characterize as unrepresentative of our ongoing operations and other noncash expenses or losses, income tax benefit, income (loss) from equity method investees and other noncash income or gains. Because adjusted EBITDA may be defined differently by other entities in our industry, our definition of this non-GAAP financial measure may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of other entities, thereby diminishing its utility.
Management uses adjusted EBITDA in making financial, operating and planning decisions and in evaluating our financial performance. Furthermore, management believes that adjusted EBITDA may provide external users of our financial statements, such as investors, commercial banks, research analysts and others, with additional meaningful comparisons between current results and results of prior periods as they are expected to be reflective of our core ongoing business.
Adjusted EBITDA is used as a supplemental financial measure to assess:
•the ability of our assets to generate cash sufficient to make future potential cash dividends and support our indebtedness;
•the financial performance of our assets without regard to financing methods, capital structure or historical cost basis;
•our operating performance and return on capital as compared to those of other entities in the midstream energy sector, without regard to financing or capital structure;
•the attractiveness of capital projects and acquisitions and the overall rates of return on alternative investment opportunities; and
•the financial performance of our assets without regard to (i) the impact of the timing of MVC shortfall payments under our gathering agreements or (ii) the timing of impairments or other income or expense items that we characterize as unrepresentative of our ongoing operations.
Adjusted EBITDA has limitations as an analytical tool and investors should not consider it in isolation or as a substitute for analysis of our results as reported under GAAP. For example:
•adjusted EBITDA includes the Company's proportionate share of Adjusted EBITDA from its unconsolidated equity method investee. Because this entity is not consolidated, the Company does not control its operations and does not have legal claim to its revenues, expenses, assets, liabilities, or cash flows, other than distributions received. As a result, this adjustment has limitations as an analytical measure and may not be comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies;
•certain items excluded from adjusted EBITDA are significant components in understanding and assessing an entity's financial performance, such as an entity's cost of capital and tax structure;

Distributable Cash Flow
We define Distributable Cash Flow as adjusted EBITDA, as defined above, less cash interest paid, cash paid for taxes, net interest expense accrued and paid on the senior notes, and maintenance capital expenditures.
Free Cash Flow
We define free cash flow as distributable cash flow attributable to common and preferred shareholders less growth capital expenditures, less investments in equity method investees, less dividends to common and preferred shareholders. Free cash flow excludes proceeds from asset sales and cash consideration paid for acquisitions.
We do not provide the GAAP financial measures of net income or loss or net cash provided by operating activities on a forward-looking basis because we are unable to predict, without unreasonable effort, certain components thereof including, but not limited to, (i) income or loss from equity method investees and (ii) asset impairments. About Summit Midstream Corporation
SMC is a value-driven corporation focused on developing, owning and operating midstream energy infrastructure assets that are strategically located in the core producing areas of unconventional resource basins, primarily shale formations, in the continental United States. SMC provides natural gas, crude oil and produced water gathering, processing and transportation services pursuant to primarily long-term, fee-based agreements with customers and counterparties in five unconventional resource basins: (i) the Williston Basin, which includes the Bakken and Three Forks shale formations in North Dakota; (ii) the Denver-Julesburg Basin, which includes the Niobrara and Codell shale formations in Colorado and Wyoming; (iii) the Fort Worth Basin, which includes the Barnett Shale formation in Texas; (iv) the Arkoma Basin, which includes the Woodford and Caney shale formations in Oklahoma; and (v) the Piceance Basin, which includes the Mesaverde formation as well as the Mancos and Niobrara shale formations in Colorado. SMC has an equity method investment in Double E Pipeline, LLC, which provides interstate natural gas transportation service from multiple receipt points in the Delaware Basin to various delivery points in and around the Waha Hub in Texas. SMC is headquartered in Houston, Texas.
